What I build
for businesses.

Most of it starts the same way: something in the business is slow, manual, or falling through the cracks. I build the thing that fixes it, put it live, and keep it running.

What I do

  • 01

    Custom software

    Software shaped around how a business already works, rather than forcing the business to change to fit an off-the-shelf tool.

    Built: a point-of-sale running daily in a bakery

  • 02

    Mobile apps

    iOS and Android from one codebase — inventory, payments, invoicing and whatever else the job actually needs.

    Built: a reseller app with Stripe and POS sync

  • 03

    Websites

    Fast, accessible sites that load properly on a phone and say what the business actually does.

    Built: this site, self-hosted and behind a tunnel

  • 04

    AI systems

    Automation that removes a real chore — answering calls, sorting enquiries, moving data between systems. Local-first where it makes sense.

    Built: a local-first agent system; an AI receptionist

  • 05

    Hosting & deployment

    Getting it live and keeping it live: servers, domains, certificates, backups, and the monitoring that tells you before a customer does.

    Doing: hosting and infrastructure as Head of Technology

  • 06

    IT support

    The unglamorous work that keeps a shop open — computers, networking and ethernet, printers and IoT devices.

    Doing: on-site support for a working bakery

How it goes

No surprises, no retainer you can’t leave.

  1. 1 · Talk

    You describe the problem. I tell you honestly whether software is the answer, and whether I’m the right person.

  2. 2 · Scope

    A written description of what gets built, what it costs, and roughly when. Fixed scope, so the price holds.

  3. 3 · Build

    You see it working early and often — not a demo at the end. Changes are easier while it’s still being made.

  4. 4 · Run it

    Deployed, backed up and monitored. Support afterwards if you want it, and no lock-in if you don’t.
